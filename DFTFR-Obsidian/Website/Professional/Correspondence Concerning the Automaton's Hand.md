An exchange of letters between myself and a colleague, occasioned by a shared unease neither of us can quite dispel: the growing habit of accepting work from a hand we did not watch move, guided by a mind — if mind is even the word — we cannot properly interrogate. The correspondence considers, without settling, the question of when such work may be trusted enough to send out into the world under our own names.

## Tom

Author and technologist Cory Doctorow says that "[AI is the asbestos we're shoveling into the walls of our civilization](https://pluralistic.net/2026/04/08/process-knowledge-vs-bosses/#wash-dishes-cut-wood) and our descendants will be digging it out for generations." I'm also worried about the technical debt caused by having humans choose to step back and let AI create code without careful oversight. I've been very careful not to distribute or sell any of the software products I've created with AI assistance, largely because if I later find out that something's wrong, I wouldn't necessarily know how to fix it. How does an experienced coder feel about using AI to create and deploy software for business?

## Bill

The fairest answer is I would not be comfortable having a single human developer or an A.I. create and deploy software on their own.  I trust that the human would not knowingly release something terribly broken.  For the A.I., I would be more worried that it did exactly what we asked of it - People are not good at making perfect requirements on the first try. Regardless of AI or not, a team needs to be involved to have the best outcome. If you are talking about having minimal programming skills, a pile of venture capitalist loot, and a penchant for AI - I think it is a risk.  Now if you have a team that knows what a good design would look like, understand how to work with an AI, and remain engaged through the process... I would be more comfortable.

  

Technical debt is a more complex question.  Most times, technical debt grows because there is a gap between what a company can afford to do and what it wants to do - not enough time or developers.  So we knowingly cut corners.  With AI we can scale capacity to get what we want done. I don’t think we will see as  much technical debt accruing with AI assisted coding.

However, the ease of which we can do this concerns me, and I think we will see a new unseen kind of technical debt. A new breed of developers that don’t know what to ask for and get something that won’t live up to the needs.  For example, saying you need a web site to sell tickets is a lot different than needing a web site that can handle 10,000 connections a minute.

That’s my take on programming.  When you look at this from a wider societal point of view, how are you thinking about it? What sort of technical debt is AI creating?
